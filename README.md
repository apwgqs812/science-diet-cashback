## Science Diet is a grid, not a single product

The single most useful thing to understand before you buy anything: Science Diet is not one dog food. It's a matrix. Hill's splits the range by life stage first, body size second, and specific health need third, and the formula names are built from those three variables.

A few naming quirks are worth knowing so you don't buy the wrong thing:

- **Science Diet** is Hill's everyday retail line, sold through pet shops, marketplaces, online pet retailers and vet clinics.
- **Prescription Diet** is the veterinary line. You need a vet's recommendation, and it's a different product family entirely. If your search results mix the two, that's why.
- **Science Plan** is the name Hill's uses for broadly the same style of range in several European and Asian markets. Same company, different label.

Hill's has been formulating on this pattern since 1939, and its own marketing leans heavily on the size of its technical bench: 220+ vets, scientists and pet nutritionists. It also claims the number one vet-recommended position, which is Hill's own claim rather than an independent finding, though it's repeated widely enough that you'll see it in most retailer listings.

Worth knowing: Hill's is owned by Colgate-Palmolive. That's not a red flag either way, but it does explain why the packaging and pricing feel more like a consumer health brand than a boutique pet food label.

## Narrowing it down: age, then size, then the specific problem

The order matters. Too many people start with the health claim on the front of the bag and buy a senior formula for a four-year-old dog.

**Age comes first.** Puppies up to around a year need a puppy formula, and pregnant or nursing dogs get switched onto puppy food too. Adult formulas cover roughly one to six years. From seven, the mature-adult range takes over, and there's an 11+ tier for small and mini breeds in their later years.

**Then body size.** Small Bites formulas use a smaller kibble for dogs who eat faster than they chew or have had teeth removed. Small Paws formulas are built around the nutrient profile of dogs that finish under about 25 pounds as adults, which is a different set of needs from a 40 kg dog.

**Then the specific issue.** This is where the range gets crowded:

- **Sensitive Stomach & Skin** targets dogs with dry, flaky or itchy skin, and leans on omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids. Hill's positions it for adults one to six with sensitive skin.
- **Perfect Digestion** uses Hill's ActivBiome+ blend, a prebiotic fibre and antioxidant mix. Hill's states the blend carries at least six times more vitamin E than AAFCO minimum requirements, and the line is built around gut microbiome support rather than an ingredient list.
- **Healthy Cuisine stews and pouches** are the wet-food side, for dogs that won't touch kibble or owners building a mixed diet.

If you're choosing between two formulas and can't tell the difference from the marketing copy, that's normal. The practical tiebreaker is your dog's actual age, weight and the one problem you're trying to solve. If there isn't a specific problem, the standard life-stage formula is the sane default.

## Two things worth knowing before you commit long term

**The recall history.** In 2019 Hill's issued a voluntary recall of certain canned dog food lots after elevated vitamin D levels were found. Vitamin D is an essential nutrient, but too much can damage kidneys. The recall covered canned product; dry formulas weren't part of it, and no active recall is in force. Hill's also runs a 100% satisfaction guarantee, so an unhappy dog is generally a refund rather than a sunk cost.

**The criticism.** It's real and you'll run into it. Science Diet uses corn and corn gluten meal in several formulas, which puts off owners who prefer grain-free or ingredient-led marketing. Protein percentages sit lower than some premium competitors. And Hill's has historically provided free food and educational material to veterinary schools, which critics argue colours how often vets recommend it. On the other side of the ledger: the formulas go through AAFCO feeding trials rather than nutrient analysis alone, the company employs credentialed veterinary nutritionists full-time, and it scores well on the WSAVA checklist criteria for evaluating pet food makers. Both things can be true at once.
